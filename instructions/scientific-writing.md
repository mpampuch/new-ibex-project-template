# Scientific Writing and Personal Voice

Read `writing/STYLE.md` when present. Treat the user's writing samples as evidence of style; do not infer detailed personal preferences without examples.

## Integrity
- Never invent data, results, quotations, citations, DOIs, p-values, sample sizes, or methods.
- Verify references against reliable bibliographic sources when available. If verification is unavailable, flag the citation instead of asserting it is correct.
- Keep observations, statistical results, interpretations, and hypotheses distinct.
- Do not strengthen causal claims beyond the study design or evidence.
- Preserve uncertainty, limitations, negative results, and relevant caveats.
- Keep values, units, gene/protein identifiers, accession numbers, and citation links intact unless a change is justified.
- Never use a source you have not actually inspected as evidence for a specific claim.

## Voice
- Follow the supplied samples for diction, sentence rhythm, paragraph structure, first-person usage, jargon, transitions, and level of formality.
- Avoid generic AI filler, exaggerated significance, stock transitions, and repetitive conclusions.
- Do not force a uniform style across blog posts and scientific manuscripts if the samples show they differ.
- Preserve the author's voice; edit only as much as the task requires.
- If no samples are available, ask for examples or state provisional style assumptions.

## Blog posts
Make the central idea clear early, define specialized terms for the intended audience, and maintain a logical narrative. Use examples only when accurate and relevant. End with a meaningful implication rather than a generic recap.

## Papers
Follow the target journal, reporting guideline, and section conventions. Methods should be reproducible; results should report what was observed; discussion should interpret findings proportionally. Check consistency between manuscript, tables, figures, supplements, and statistical values.

## Editing workflow
1. Identify audience, purpose, target venue, and requested editing depth.
2. Preserve the author's factual claims and intended meaning.
3. Improve clarity, precision, flow, and structure.
4. Check consistency of terminology, numbers, units, and citations.
5. List substantive changes and flag unresolved factual questions.
6. Do not claim references or facts were verified unless they were checked.

## Style calibration
When the user supplies writing samples:
- Analyze each genre separately if appropriate.
- Extract a concise style profile into `writing/STYLE.md`.
- Keep a few short, representative excerpts locally if permitted; avoid unnecessary full-text duplication.
- Test the profile by revising a paragraph and asking the author whether it sounds like them.
